# Writing Research Articles — 3. Methods

**Source:** https://writinginthesciences.uoguelph.ca/methods/

## The reader's question

**What was done, and how was it done?**

## Three main components

1. **Materials:** Describe equipment, materials, participants, organisms, sites, or samples at the level needed to understand the study.
2. **Procedures:** Give the study design and the actual sequence of work. Include relevant collection, preparation, storage, setup, and data-acquisition details. Chronological order is often useful.
3. **Statistical Analysis:** Describe analyses performed, where applicable. Report their outcomes in Results.

## Five writing techniques

1. **Be specific.** Provide enough relevant detail for other researchers to evaluate or replicate the study.
2. **Consider the audience.** Explain unusual apparatus or techniques. Use less space for equipment familiar to the target readers.
3. **Avoid repetition.** Cite established published procedures when appropriate and explain modifications made in this study.
4. **Use photos where helpful.** Consult photographs to reconstruct equipment or field details. A Methods schematic can clarify an apparatus; outcome figures belong in Results.
5. **Write in the past tense.** Describe actions already performed rather than giving lab-manual commands.

## Completion criteria

The Methods reflect the performed study. Their detail matches the target audience and supports interpretation or replication. All stated sample counts, equipment specifications, and analyses have documentary support.
