# Writing Research Articles — 2. Introduction

**Source:** https://writinginthesciences.uoguelph.ca/introductions/

## The reader's question

**What is the research question, and why was it investigated?**

The course presents the Introduction as a funnel: move from a broad topic toward a specific purpose statement.

## Four main components

1. **Topic:** Give the background readers need to understand the study. Select information relevant to the research question.
2. **Problem:** Explain the problem and why it matters. Use key prior studies to show how researchers have approached it; reserve a full literature review for contexts that need one.
3. **Gap:** Identify what prior work has not established. Specify a missing mechanism, approach, process, scope, population, or perspective where relevant.
4. **Purpose Statement:** State the study's precise purpose as a declarative statement rather than only a question.

## Hypotheses and objectives

- Include hypotheses and predictions when they belong to the actual design and the disciplinary convention.
- Descriptive or exploratory studies can state their purpose without inventing a prior hypothesis.
- State specific objectives when helpful. Keep the overarching Purpose Statement visible.
- Choose verbs that accurately describe the study's investigated relationships.

## Five writing techniques

1. **Move from broad to specific.** Narrow the background toward the immediate research problem.
2. **Move from known to new.** Start a sentence or paragraph with an established concept, then build toward new information. A term from one sentence can become the starting point of the next.
3. **Use active voice where useful.** Make the actor clear. Retain passive constructions when they serve the journal's conventions or information focus.
4. **Write topic sentences.** State the main function or claim of each paragraph.
5. **Write concluding sentences.** Explain the significance of the paragraph and prepare the next idea.

## Completion criteria

The reader can trace the problem from the topic to the knowledge gap. The Purpose Statement matches the actual study. Each paragraph advances the funnel and connects to the next paragraph.
