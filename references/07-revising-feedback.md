# Writing Research Articles — 7. Revising and Responding to Feedback

**Source:** https://writinginthesciences.uoguelph.ca/revising-and-responding-to-feedback/

## Revision and editing

- **Revision** examines content, argument, organization, and overall direction.
- **Editing** improves language, grammar, formatting, citation details, and sentence clarity once the structure is sound.

## Revision strategies

1. Set the draft aside briefly when the schedule permits.
2. Read the full draft and take notes before moving text.
3. Compare the draft with its original outline. Create a reverse outline when the structure has changed.
4. Focus each revision pass on one dimension, such as content or structure.
5. Keep earlier versions and seek feedback from colleagues, supervisors, or writing consultants.

## Reverse outlining

For **each paragraph**, record:

1. The paragraph's topic and contribution to the research question.
2. Its topic sentence and how clearly that sentence states the point.

Then evaluate:

- **Logical order:** Does each paragraph occupy the most useful position?
- **Transitions:** Do topic sentences and linking sentences connect the ideas?
- **Paragraph length:** Should an overloaded paragraph be divided? Should a thin paragraph be developed, moved, or removed?

The course sets no universal paragraph-length threshold. Reverse outlining is also useful for integrating sections drafted by different coauthors.

## Editing and self-editing

After structural revision, examine coherence, precise wording, concision, grammar, format, and references. Edit in focused passes. Reading aloud may expose awkward phrasing. Assess automated suggestions rather than accepting them indiscriminately.

## Responding to feedback

1. Read all feedback and identify any requests that require clarification or additional evidence.
2. Group comments by issue and prioritize content and structure before style.
3. Track each comment, planned action, actual change, location, and response in `../assets/feedback-response-log.md`.
4. Check the complete response record before resubmission. Record the rationale when an editorial suggestion is not adopted.

The course refers to review-matrix and feedback-chart resources, including work by Raúl Pacheco-Vega, Megan Poorman, and the University of Guelph.

## Completion criteria

The revision addresses argument and structure before polishing language. The reverse outline contains one entry per paragraph. Each substantive reviewer comment has a traceable response and manuscript location.
