# Writing Research Articles — 6. Titles and Abstracts

**Source:** https://writinginthesciences.uoguelph.ca/titles-abstracts/

## Five steps for an effective title

1. **Anticipate keywords.** Include terms a relevant reader would search for.
2. **Indicate the major focus.** One possible structure names an independent variable, a dependent variable, and an object of study. Use it when it fits the design.
3. **Be specific and comprehensive.** Identify the precise behavior, subject, group, setting, or scope when it improves meaning.
4. **Avoid unnecessary words.** Remove generic introductions, nominalizations, and redundant phrases while retaining key indexing terms.
5. **Consider phrasing.** A title may name the topic, ask a question, or state the main finding. Compare titles in the target journal.

## Written abstract

A written abstract stands alone and lets readers assess the article quickly. It reports the answer and its significance rather than withholding the result.

A practical sequence uses the article's IMRAD logic:

1. Background and research purpose; hypothesis if relevant.
2. Essential Methods.
3. Main Results.
4. Research answer, interpretation, and contribution.

- Ensure that Results and contribution have space within the journal's word limit.
- Draw each statement from the completed manuscript. Revise an early abstract after the article is drafted.
- Follow any required structured-abstract headings.
- References to other authors or to article figures and tables are generally omitted from abstracts.

The storytelling module also offers **ABT** as a way to organize an abstract around known information, the problem, and the supported answer. Use `08-storytelling.md` when ABT is requested.

## Graphical and visual abstracts

Some journals publish graphical or video abstracts alongside a written abstract. These formats supplement the written abstract; they do not replace it. A graphical abstract summarizes the main findings in one image. The course describes diagram-style and visual-style approaches.

The visual-abstract example contains a title, visual elements that represent methods or findings, and an article citation. Follow the journal's submission requirements, including the expected number of findings.

## Completion criteria

The title is searchable, specific, and concise. The abstract states purpose, methods, actual results, and the contribution. Any graphical-abstract plan reflects the same evidence and the journal's format.
