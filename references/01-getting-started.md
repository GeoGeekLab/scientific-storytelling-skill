# Writing Research Articles — 1. Getting Started

**Source:** https://writinginthesciences.uoguelph.ca/getting-started/  
**Module authors:** Jodie Salter and Sarah Gibbons

## Purpose of a research article

Research articles share methods, results, data, interpretations, and possible future work with scientific peers. They also inform readers outside research, including policymakers. Peer review is part of scientific publication. Clear writing helps different readers evaluate and use the work.

## Select a target journal

Identify the journal before writing when possible. The target journal helps define the audience and the expected genre.

Consider:

- The journal's selectivity and competitiveness.
- Its impact factor and disciplinary relevance.
- Open-access status and applicable funder requirements.
- Access through libraries and other distribution channels.
- Typical time to a publication decision.
- Advice from supervisors and journals that publish frequently cited work in the field.

When the user has selected a journal, use it as the reference point. When the user requests journal selection, compare the available candidates using these factors and the user's priorities.

## Conduct a genre analysis

Read at least three representative articles from the target journal when they are available. Read for form as well as content. Answer **all four course questions** for each article:

1. How is each section organized?
2. What function does each paragraph perform?
3. Which signal phrases introduce key information?
4. How does the writer transition to a new topic?

Compare the articles for repeated conventions and useful variations. Separate observations about each article from conclusions about the journal. Record any unhelpful writing choices the reader might avoid.

Use `../assets/genre-analysis.md` for the comparison. If articles are not supplied or accessible, use the worksheet to specify what evidence is needed.

## Use the IMRAD convention

| Section | Reader's question |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | What is the research question, and why investigate it? |
| Methods | What did the researchers do, and how? |
| Results | What did they find, and which patterns or trends emerged? |
| Discussion | What do the findings mean, and how do they relate to work in the field? |

IMRAD is a common framework. Some journals combine Results and Discussion. Use genre analysis to identify the relevant version.

## Map and draft

The course presents a Research Article (or Book) Mapping Template to plan sections and their contents. Adapt `../assets/research-article-map.md` to the target journal and study.

Drafting order can differ from reading order:

- Start with Methods when procedures are clear and writing can begin before analysis is complete.
- Start with Results when tables, figures, and reported patterns are ready.
- Revisit the Introduction, Discussion, title, and abstract as the article's focus becomes clearer.

## Completion criteria

The plan names its intended journal or journal-selection criteria. Genre findings use inspected articles and the four questions. The article map addresses the IMRAD reader questions and observed journal conventions.
