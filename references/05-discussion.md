# Writing Research Articles — 5. Discussion

**Source:** https://writinginthesciences.uoguelph.ca/discussion/

## The reader's question

**What do the findings mean, and how do they relate to research in the field?**

## Six main components

1. **Research Answer:** Answer the stated research question directly. If a hypothesis was tested, describe whether the evidence supports it.
2. **Key Findings:** Summarize the results that matter most to the answer.
3. **Interpretations:** Explain what the findings mean. Ground the explanation in the observed evidence.
4. **Comparison to Other Studies:** Compare results with relevant work. Explain the meaning of agreements and differences.
5. **Acknowledgement of Limitations:** Name specific limits on the methods, evidence, or interpretation.
6. **Recommendations for Future Research:** Indicate studies that could extend or test the findings.

## Typical organization

- **Opening:** Answer the research question and state the main findings.
- **Body:** Interpret each key finding and compare it with the literature. The course describes both result-first and literature-first paragraph sequences, with a preference for foregrounding the study's results.
- **Closing:** Address limitations and future research, then articulate the study's contribution. Some journals use a separate Conclusion.

The Discussion generally moves from specific findings to wider significance, the reverse direction of the Introduction's funnel.

## Writing techniques

1. **Specify limitations.** Relate each limitation to its effect on inference or generalizability.
2. **Conclude with contributions.** Show what the work adds at the level warranted by its evidence.
3. **Use signal phrases.** Mark answers, comparisons, limitations, and implications.
4. **Choose verbs precisely.** Select *suggests*, *indicates*, *shows*, or suitably qualified language according to evidentiary strength. Reserve strong causal language for designs that support causation.

## Disciplinary variation

- Results and Discussion can share one section. Use clear internal signals between observation and explanation.
- Some studies, including mixed-methods research, integrate multiple evidence types in the Discussion.
- Follow the target journal for the role and placement of a Conclusion.

## Completion criteria

The answer addresses the same question posed in the Introduction. Interpretations link to results. Comparisons cite identifiable studies. Limitations are specific. The final contribution matches the evidence.
